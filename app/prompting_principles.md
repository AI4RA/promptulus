### Prompt Engineering Principles

| Advice Category | Principle | Why This Works | What It Is | Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1: Core Clarity** | **Be Specific & Constrained** | Reduces guesswork so the model stops filling gaps with assumptions. Explaining the why behind a constraint helps it honor the intent, not just the letter. | State goal, length, audience, format, and key requirements, with reasons (e.g., "Summarize this NOFO in 5 bullets for a busy department chair; skip eligibility, we already qualify"). Colleague test: if a colleague with minimal context would be confused, the model will be too. | Too many constraints box in the answer or conflict. Precision on length can crowd out completeness. |
| **Level 1: Core Clarity** | **Define the Role** | Sets voice and perspective so the draft sounds right for its reader. | Name the perspective to write from (e.g., "write as a sponsored programs officer replying to a PI about an unallowable cost"). Use it for tone and viewpoint, not accuracy. | Expert personas do not improve accuracy: Zheng et al. (2024) found no reliable gain across 162 roles, and Wharton's December 2025 study (which tested Gemini 2.5 Flash) found the same, with out-of-domain experts triggering refusals. "You are a world-class expert" is wasted words. |
| **Level 1: Core Clarity** | **Specify the Target Audience** | Tailors vocabulary, detail, and tone to the person who will actually read the result. | Name the reader (e.g., "a new PI who has never built an NIH budget," "the dean's office," "a sponsor program officer"). | An unclear audience gets a generic reader by default; one that conflicts with the task muddles the output. |
| **Level 1: Core Clarity** | **Plain, Calm Language** | As of October 2026 the Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic guides all say shouting, bribes, threats, and pleading are unnecessary or counterproductive; forceful wording can make the model over-apply a rule. | Write as you would to a capable colleague: no ALL CAPS, no "MUST" or "CRITICAL", no "I'll tip you", no persuasive framing. Say it once, plainly. | Calm does not mean vague. If an instruction keeps getting missed, add a reason or an example, not more emphasis. |
| **Level 2: Structure & Input Design** | **Minimize Irrelevant Context** | Keeps the model focused and less likely to latch onto a stray detail. | Include only what the model needs. For a budget justification, paste the budget lines and the sponsor's cost rules, not the whole proposal. | People often cut context that mattered; a little extra usually hurts less than too little. (Mnemos covers what to include.) |
| **Level 2: Structure & Input Design** | **Show Good Examples and State Positive Instructions** | Models follow "do this" more reliably than "don't do that." As of October 2026, Google's Gemini 3 guide says broad negative constraints get dropped, and Anthropic's lead rule is say what to do, not what not to do. | Replace "don't be vague" with "name the cost category and the regulation section." Show a good example (a well-written subaward checklist item), not a bad one to avoid. | One example can anchor the model to its wording. A short, specific negative is fine ("omit the PI's salary figure"). |
| **Level 2: Structure & Input Design** | **Mark the Parts and Put the Question Last** | Boundaries keep your instructions separate from pasted material, and a request at the end is easier to act on after reading everything above it (Google and Anthropic guides, as of October 2026). | Use markdown headings or XML-style tags as delimiters: `<sponsor_email> ... </sponsor_email>` then `<task> Draft a short reply confirming the no-cost extension </task>`. Long pasted text first, question last. | A little setup. Tags must open and close consistently, with descriptive names. |
| **Level 3: Reasoning & Technique** | **Describe the Outcome, Not the Thinking** | As of October 2026, Gemini, Claude, and OpenAI's current models reason internally before answering. Describing a good result gives that reasoning a target. | Instead of "think step by step," say what the end product must contain: "Check this RPPR narrative against the three required sections and list anything missing." | As of October 2026: Google says step-by-step prompting is generally not necessary for Gemini 2.5 and 3, and Gemini 3 may over-analyze verbose older techniques; OpenAI says to avoid chain-of-thought prompts with reasoning models; Anthropic treats manual chain-of-thought as a fallback when thinking is off. "Show your reasoning" yields an after-the-fact explanation, not the internal process. |
| **Level 3: Reasoning & Technique** | **Few-Shot Examples** | Shows rather than tells, the most reliable way to convey format, tone, and level of detail. | Give 3 to 5 examples with identical formatting (Anthropic and Google, as of October 2026), e.g., three past budget justification paragraphs, then "write the equipment paragraph in the same style." | Current reasoning models often need no examples for simple tasks (OpenAI, as of October 2026). A weak example teaches a weak pattern; inconsistent formatting teaches confusion. |
| **Level 3: Reasoning & Technique** | **Explicit Output Format** | Produces predictable output you can drop straight into a memo, email, or spreadsheet. | Name the shape: "a table with columns Cost Item, Amount, Justification," "a 150-word email," "a numbered checklist of subaward documents." | Rigid formats break when content does not fit; the model may trim accuracy to satisfy the format. |
| **Level 3: Reasoning & Technique** | **Set Clear Evaluation Criteria** | The model can only hit a target you describe. Named criteria also make a later self-check meaningful. | Define success up front: "must cite the 2 CFR 200 section," "under 200 words," "every deadline in the NOFO appears in the summary." | Vague criteria ("make it good") are ignored. Contradictory ones ("exhaustive" and "one paragraph") force the model to pick. |
| **Level 4: Refinement & Evaluation** | **Ask for a Self-Check Against Named Criteria Before Finishing** | A targeted review pass catches omissions a single draft misses, and works far better with named criteria than with "check your work." | After the task, add: "Before finishing, check the draft: every NOFO deadline included, no cost over the sponsor cap, neutral tone. Fix any misses, then give the final version." | Self-review is not independent verification; the model can confirm its own mistakes. Use Veridex's methods for anything consequential. |
| **Level 4: Refinement & Evaluation** | **Ask for Options Before a Decision** | Surfaces alternatives and trade-offs before you commit, useful when there is a real judgment call. | "Give me three ways to structure the cost share for this subaward, with pros and cons of each, then recommend one." | Built-in thinking already weighs alternatives internally for most tasks, so this mainly helps when you need to see the options yourself. Long output, and you still decide. |
| **Level 4: Refinement & Evaluation** | **State a Few Specific Rules** | A short list of concrete rules gets remembered and applied; a long or blanket list does not. | Give 3 to 5 specific, positive rules (e.g., "Use the sponsor's cost-category names," "Cite the policy section for each disallowed cost," "Keep each item to one sentence"). | Blanket rules like "never make up facts" over-apply and can cause refusals or hedging. Rules can conflict ("be brief" vs. "cite everything"). |
| **Level 5: Meta-Thinking & Customization** | **Meta-Prompting** | The model knows how prompts work and can turn a rough request into a complete one faster than you can. | Ask the model to draft or improve the prompt: "I want a NOFO summary for our faculty newsletter. Ask what is missing, then write the prompt you would use." Save the result as a template for recurring work. | The model may add detail you never wanted, so read the generated prompt before using it. Templates drift when sponsor requirements change. |

Sources (as of October 2026): Anthropic prompting best practices; Google Gemini prompting strategies, Gemini 3 guide, Prompting with thinking; OpenAI reasoning best practices; Wharton GAIL expert personas study (Dec 2025); Zheng et al. 2024 (arXiv 2311.10054).
